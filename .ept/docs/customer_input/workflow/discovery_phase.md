
# Discovery Phase

## What and why is the Discovery phase required?

Definition: The definition of the discovery phase stands for comprehensive research to collect as much as possible information in order to plan the entire digital project. The main role of the discovery phase is to understand the ins and outs of the product and provide the answers to the following questions:

- What are the current challenges in the specific market?
- Who is the target audience?
- What are the different needs/requirements of the audience?
- What is the final goal of the digital product?
- What pain points will be solved with the product?
- How will this product stand out from the competition?
- What will be the functionality of the product?
- What platform/ tech stack are we building the application for?

## Focus area for discovery phase

Despite providing the purpose and the direction of the project, the discovery has a few more benefits. Let’s see why you need a discovery phase for product development. 

### Focus on business objectives

- Help ensure a higher return on investment 
- How proposed solution matches the company vision 
- Provide your project manager with any existing information or documentation about the project

### Focus on user need

- Create a user-oriented experience.
- Solution that solve their pain point
- Mapping the user journey 

### Focus on the right solution

- Better identify project scope and goals resulting in a more accurate estimate
- Involve in-house specialists at an early stage to maximize the impact of their familiarity with the problem to be solved

### Focus on the context (Product team)

- Make design decisions based on data, not assumptions.
- Make use of the discovery phase to avoid costly changes during advanced stages of the development process

## The 6 agile product discovery phases

The product discovery phase is descriptive of the iterative processes involved in taking away the uncertainties surrounding an idea to ensure that the right solution or product is developed for the appropriate audience. It gives product teams more confidence going forward and is the foundation to the success of the implementation and launching phases later. There are different phases involved in product discovery. These phases are: 

- Researching and understanding user problems
- Ideating solutions
- Creating prototypes
- Creating alignment & shared understanding among internal & external stakeholders
- Validating your ideas
- Refining results

### 1. Researching and understanding user problems

User requirement:

- What do users need?
- Issues or struggles of the user.
- What is already working and what isn’t?
- 
Typically, research can be further classified as a primary research or secondary research. 
Appropriate methods to be used at this stage include 1:1 user interviews, focus groups, competitive analysis, benchmark studies, and ethnographic research. The team involved in conducting research: The product owner in coordination with the Product manager.

### 2. Ideating solutions

- After developing a basic understanding of the users’ problems, you can start to think of solutions.
- Every team member should be given some time to think over the problem and propose ideas to mitigate the issue.
- The primary goal is for everyone to think big and leave their comfort zones. 
- Decision to finalize the solution depends on multiple factors like feasibility, cost, efforts, timeline, other dependencies, etc. 

At this phase, we as a team need to reach for the sky and let reality catch up. Also, try to democratize our strategy within the group, rather than one person picking what they like. The entire team is involved in this phase.

### 3. Creating prototypes

- By this phase, you should have a variety of ideas and a rough estimate of resources (budget & timing). So, you know which solutions are feasible.
- Prototyping tools are like “shiny objects” for product discovery, so you may not need to use them. You should instead focus on stimulating an experience that validates the ideas you are facing. 
- Product owner along with UI team actively participate in this phase.

### 4. Creating alignment & shared understanding among internal & external stakeholders

- This phase involves ensuring alignment in product management across different levels of your organization. 
- It is crucial to have clarity about product delivery from the start, especially if you work in a larger organization.
- Some stakeholders may come up with features that they want to see in the product. Although some may understand that you need to build an MVP first, it is possible that they still insist on storing the idea or product in the backlog. 
- Product manager along with Product owner is actively involved with stakeholders to finalize MVP 

### 5. Validating your ideas

Before implementation, your ideas and hypotheses should always go through a validation

- Create a roadmap to let stakeholders know what's on your radar in development
- Gather insights on planned features
- Allow your power users to vote and comment on ideas
- 
The entire team along with other project stakeholders are involved at this stage.

### 6. Refining results

Product discovery is not usually a linear process. You may need to take some steps back during your validation stage or after it. Whether you are only picking newer ideas from your ideation stage to test, or you are returning to the initial phase of research, going back can be painful. Still, it makes more sense than going forward without anything tangible. 
The entire team along with other project stakeholders are involved at this stage.

## Benefits of discovery phase

The major benefit of the discovery phase consulting is that the project requirements and specifications can be validated based on the evidence, not on assumptions. Consequently, a balance between the business goal, and the needs and wishes of the users in advance. 

- Reduce risk: Missed deadline risk is reduced by 75% and overall cost savings can exceed 50%
- Establish a roadmap: You get a full timeline of the project with the interim goals, deliverables and deadlines 
- Build trust: Regular communication and in-depth discussion will establish a two-way trust

## The Outcome of the discovery

The following artifacts can be provided as deliverables:

- Approved Software requirements specification (SRS): This is a document fully describing the project goals, feature set, recommended tech stack, and architecture outline. It also includes project dependencies, risks, and dependent systems
- Approved and prioritized discovery Feature list\Backlog;
- Non-functional requirements (as a part of SAD);
- Implementation Roadmap;
- Solution architecture document (SAD);
- When applicable: Wireframes (less frequently designs) and personas, UX journeys;
- Results of UX research and customers interviews (optional);
- Vision and Scope document or log with assumptions, dependencies and constraints (as a part of SAD); 
- Resource plan;

## Discovery Phase Team: Its Roles and Responsibilities

As soon as pre-discovery is done, and the budget is determined we build a Discovery team to help achieve our objectives. The team usually has four experts – A Product Manager, a Product owner, a Solution Architect, a UI/UX designer.

### Product Manager

- Manages the entire product lifecycle and product roadmap
- Works with the engineering team on core specifications and product functionality
- Creating alignment & shared understanding among internal & external stakeholders.
- He addresses project roadblocks. 

### Product Owner

- To participate in the discovery phase i.e., research, prototype creation, etc. 
- To create and take signoff on the SRS document 
- To create and define epic backlog and all the items need to be mentioned elaborately.
- To Prioritize work items and product backlog, this must be in line with customer vision and goals

### Solution Architect

- The Solution Architect is responsible for analyzing the technology environment and the performance, scalability, and maintainability of the product. 
- Solution architects also investigate third-party frameworks and platforms, along with their risks and benefits.

### UI/UX Team

- These team member is in charge of creating a user experience in keeping with the aims of the project. 
- This is likely to include easy navigation, intuitive design, and a visually attractive project. 
- To make wireframes and prototypes to bring the product vision to life and make sure target users understand how to use the software.
